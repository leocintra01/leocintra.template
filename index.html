Payments: the growth avenue and where it comes from

Payments have accelerated consistently over the last quarters and became the main contributor to marginal growth, and in our view the sustainability of this pace is the main point of discussion among investors. TPV rose from BRL 6.0 billion in 2022 to BRL 10.9 billion in 2025, and reached BRL 3.9 billion in 2Q26 alone, up 54% YoY and 36% organically. In this context, the debate has been less about the direction of the business and more about when recently announced clients start contributing to volume, since the quarter brought a relevant set of new names, including Aegea in sanitation, Vitru and FMU in higher education, Qualicorp in health and Apsa in condominiums, which in our view carry relevant TPV potential over the coming years.

Margins: operating leverage and take-rate dynamics

The margin trajectory, in our view, reflects the balance between two forces that work in opposite directions. On the positive side, the model requires limited incremental structure to grow, so the dilution of fixed costs as payments scale flows through to profitability, a dynamic already visible in the adjusted EBITDA margin moving from 33% in 2024 to 34% in 2025 and 35% in 2Q26, with capex declining from 8.6% of net revenue to around 6% in the first half of 2026 and cash conversion rising from 74% to 82%. Adding to this, new AI solutions could allow the company to support growth in the coming years with a slower pace of headcount expansion. On the other side, the take rate faces natural pressure as Pix gains weight in the mix, a secular trend that should continue, and as contracts are renegotiated over time. In this context, we see the net effect as a gradual margin gain in the coming years, reaching [XX]% by 2028.

At the same time, the distribution has been compatible with the investment agenda.
