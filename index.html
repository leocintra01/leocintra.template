Unifique — Controlling Shareholder Announces Delisting Tender Offer

Unifique (FIQE3) — [Rating], TP BRL [XX] (YE[XX]). Flash News, [XX] October 2026. Prices as of October 2, 2026; BRL unless noted. Draft for internal review.

The News: Unifique announced that its controlling shareholder, Unitá Participações, intends to launch a unified tender offer at BRL 6.00/share to convert the company's registration with the CVM from category "A" to "B" and to exit B3's Novo Mercado. In practice, the conversion ends the trading of Unifique's shares on B3, while preserving its status as a reporting company able to issue non-equity securities, such as debentures. If launched, the offer will target all shares not held by Unitá or in treasury, although only the ~99.7 million shares in free float (~25% of the capital, or ~BRL 598 million at the offer price) count toward the success quorum (see Exhibit 1).

The offer: the price implies a total equity value of ~BRL 2,337 million (excluding treasury shares), representing a premium of ~10% over the October 2 closing price, and is above the BRL 5.42/share fair value calculated by the independent appraiser based on the VWAP methodology. That said, the implied EV of ~BRL 3,088 million represents ~4.6x EV/EBITDA LTM (2Q26) and ~3.9x EV/EBITDA 2027E, based on our estimates (see our latest update on the company here). As a reference, the offer implies a discount of ~25% to the multiple paid by Claro in the recent acquisition of a regional ISP (6.2x EV/EBITDA LTM), which may be a point of attention for minority shareholders. Importantly, the offer remains subject to registration with the CVM, B3's authorization for the auction and other applicable regulatory approvals.

Scenarios and next steps: the outcome will depend on the acceptance level among free-float shareholders, while visibility on the timeline is still limited. Both the conversion of registration and the exit from the Novo Mercado are subject to minimum acceptance levels among the free-float shareholders who register for the auction (more than two-thirds and more than one-third, respectively). In other words, should holders of one-third or more of the registered shares not accept the offer, the conversion would not take place and the company would remain listed (see Exhibit 2). As disclosed in the material fact, the 15-day period for a potential request to call a special meeting of free-float shareholders started on October 2, and the next steps include the CVM's analysis of the registration request and the publication of the offer document, which should detail the terms of the transaction, including the auction date. We will continue to monitor the developments closely.
