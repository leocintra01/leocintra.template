Bemobi — Four themes that should define the story ahead

Bemobi has gradually reshaped its business over the past five years, evolving from carrier billing into a vertical payments platform for recurring services. Payments and SaaS accounted for 69.5% of net revenue in 2Q26, with payments alone at 49.3%, and the company reports serving 16 of the 20 largest recurring-service companies in Brazil. Rather than focusing on quarterly dynamics, in this report we discuss four themes that, in our view, should define the story over the coming years: i) the growth prospects for TPV in payments; ii) the margin trajectory, balancing operating leverage against mix and take-rate dynamics; iii) the return on invested capital, which we split between the consolidated business and payments; and iv) capital allocation. Payments: growth prospects supported by the existing client base

Payments volume has compounded consistently, with growth holding up even as the base becomes larger. TPV rose from BRL 6.0 billion in 2022 to BRL 10.9 billion in 2025, and reached BRL 3.9 billion in 2Q26 alone, up 54% YoY and 36% organically. In our view, the nature of the opportunity helps to explain this resilience, since Bemobi monetizes bills that are already being issued, which makes growth more dependent on the migration of existing volume to digital channels than on new consumption. Margins: operating leverage against mix and take-rate dynamics

The margin has been drifting higher as revenue grows faster than the cost base, with capex intensity falling at the same time. The adjusted EBITDA margin moved from 33% in 2024 to 34% in 2025 and 35% in 2Q26, while capex declined from 8.6% of net revenue in 2024 to 7.8% in 2025 and to around 6% in the first half of 2026, lifting cash conversion from 74% to 82% over the same period. Take-rate dynamics remain the main counterpoint to the thesis, and the record so far suggests volume has been the offset. The disclosed take rate moved from 3.0% in 2022 and 2023 to 2.6% in 2024 and 2025, and to 2.4% in 2Q26, which the company attributes to the Paytime mix and to a health client whose transactions are concentrated in Pix. Over the same period, payments revenue rose from BRL 178 million in 2022 to BRL 286 million in 2025, as an 82% increase in TPV more than offset the compression. Against this backdrop, Bemobi has been adding payment methods and higher-value products, including installments, card wallets and loyalty points, and Pix Automático was already live with five banks and 16 clients in 1Q26. ROIC: comparing the returns of the consolidated business and payments

The asset-light profile supports a healthy return on invested capital, and this is the lens we use to compare the two businesses within Bemobi. Capex has been running at around 6% of net revenue, largely directed to intangible assets, which limits the fixed capital required to scale the platform and supports a consolidated ROIC of [XX]% in [XX], against a cost of capital of [XX]%. In our view, this spread offers a useful measure of the quality of the transformation carried out since the IPO.

The return profile of payments, in turn, differs from that of the consolidated business. At the consolidated level, the goodwill from the acquisitions completed over the last five years weighs on capital employed and dilutes the reported return. In payments, capital employed is essentially working capital, with gateway receivables on one side and amounts payable to billers on the other, funded with the company's own resources. Taken together, these characteristics justify looking at the two separately, and our calculation compares both with the cost of capital.

Capital allocation: growth, distribution and M&A funded from operating cash flow

Bemobi has combined shareholder remuneration with a sequence of acquisitions while continuing to fund the growth of its payments operation. Since the IPO, the company has returned BRL 467 million to shareholders, equivalent to around 55% of the net proceeds raised in the offering, with BRL 265 million of dividends paid in 2025 alone, and has committed to distributing 100% of 2026 net income. Over the same period, it invested BRL 279 million in acquisitions and BRL 231 million in capex, while the float payable to billers grew from BRL 45 million in 2022 to BRL 444 million in 2025 as TPV scaled. Looking ahead, the discussion in our view is less about the sustainability of the distribution and more about how the company balances distribution, M&A and the capital employed in payments.

The way payments working capital is funded is, in itself, a capital allocation decision. The company has stated that it funds this operation with its own resources on the view that the capital employed generates a return above its average, which is precisely what our ROIC calculation sets out to test. Looking ahead, funding these volumes through a receivables fund would free up capital and alter the return profile of the business, an alternative that in our view becomes more relevant as TPV scales. On balance, the buyback carried out in May also reduced the size of the swap position, which should lower the volatility of the financial result in the coming quarters.
